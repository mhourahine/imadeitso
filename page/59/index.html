recipe: squash spice loaf

Posted on November 9, 2010 by imadeitso

i’ve made this recipe in the past using butternut squash, buttercup squash as well as a small pumpkin for the purée. i imagine any sweet squash would work. i used acorn squash (corey corrected me in the comments below, she will now be forever known as the queen of squash) to make this loaf. different squashes have different moisture content and the cooking time may vary.

also, i should say i’m not sure who to give credit to for this recipe. it’s one of those that i’ve tweaked and re-copied over the years and i really don’t know where the original came from. the first part shows how i make the purée and the second explains how i use the purée to make the loaf.  here goes…

i make the purée by slicing the squash in half and scooping out the seeds with a spoon — don’t throw those seeds out! they’re arguably the best part, and i’ll share how i roast them another time.

i place the squash halves face-down on a parchment lined baking sheet, then into the oven at 425 F (about 220 C) for approximately 30 minutes.

a fork should pierce the skin easily when the squash is cooked throughout, so depending on the variety of squash used, i keep testing with a fork every few minutes. once the squash is baked through, i take it out of the oven and let it cool.  then i use a spoon to scoop the flesh away from the skin and into a bowl.

you can use a food processor to puree, but i just whisk it with a fork til i have a fairly mushy smooth consistency. i think “mushy smooth” are technical culinary terms, right?… it doesn’t really matter because you’ve almost got this:

oops, did i give away the good stuff too soon? it’s quite good. here’s how you use the purée in the recipe to make this lovely loaf of spicy warm goodness.

ingredients:

  • 1 cup chopped walnuts (i roast these first to bring out their flavour, but using them at all is optional)
  • 2 cups of squash purée as prepared above
  • 3 eggs
  • 1 cup packed brown sugar (i’ve gotten away with even less sugar, some squash are sweet enough, and i use unsweetened applesauce in place of oil so even that adds a little more sweetness to the mix)
  • 1 cup unsweetened applesauce (oh hello you! i was just talking about you.)
  • 1 cup all purpose flour + 1 cup whole wheat flour (alternatively, you could use 2 cups white flour, or 2 cups whole wheat flour, but you’ll need to adjust the amounts of wet ingredients, so either increase or decrease the amount of squash and/or applesauce. generally speaking, more whole wheat flour requires more wet ingredients to create a moist mixture, whereas white flour requires less.)
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 ground ginger
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt

directions:

  • in one bowl, whisk the eggs, add sugar and applesauce and keep mixing til smooth.
  • in another bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt and the rest of the spices.
  • once the flour mixture is blended, add it to the egg mixture in portions, alternating with the squash purée. so, add about 1/3 of the flour into the egg mixture, stir, add about 1/3 of the squash purée, stir, add another 1/3 of the flour, mix, add another 1/3 of the squash, stir and so on.  if you are adding walnuts, fold them into the mix at the end, and pour it into a lightly greased loaf pan.
  • bake in the oven at 350 F (about 180 C) for about 30 minutes, until a toothpick or bamboo skewer inserted into the middle of the loaf comes out clean. let cool and enjoy.

oops. how did that icing get on there? enter part 3 of this recipe.  totally not needed, as this loaf is pretty good on its own, but if you’re making a treat for someone with a sweet tooth…

easy icing recipe:

using a mixer, beat 8oz (250g) light cream cheese with 1/4 cup unsalted butter until blended and smooth. add 1/3 cup of brown sugar and about a teaspoon or two of grated orange or lemon or lime rind for zing. another technical culinary term, i’m sure.

zing!

enjoy.

free pdf of arts and crafts supply labels

Posted on November 4, 2010 by imadeitso

with the arrival of autumn, my attention has turned to the indoors, and i’ve been spending some time re-organizing our craft and creative supplies. by “our” i mean mine and my kids’, there is a lot of overlap there as you can tell from some of the projects i’ve shared.

it has been slow-going, but i enjoy this sort of thing and not just because i adore my label maker ;-)

i know some people are most creative in a messy environment, while others need absolute order before they can focus. i used to be at the latter end of the scale, til i had children. now i’m somewhere in the middle. i’ve had to let a lot of my standards slide, and in the end, i think i’m a little more relaxed/less neurotic because of it.  i mean, when the labels of the bottles in my fridge didn’t all face the same way for the first time, and the world kept spinning along normally, well… it’s just interesting how things (ie. how i) have changed over the years.

but the messy situation was reaching critical, and with the fun summer behind us, a sort and tidy was in order. i drew my own images to label the bins (shh, don’t tell my neglected label maker) using adobe ideas . (adobe ideas is a free application for the iphone and ipad, and i’ve previously written about how i used it to create my blog header and other images.)

14 reasons i love vintage pyrex

Posted on November 1, 2010 by imadeitso

1. the colours.

{the above photo is courtesy of madge, author of solid cherry blog, and owner of way more beautiful pyrex than i can ever hope to find.}

2. the designs.

3. old pyrex wasn’t made in china (sorry china). i have pieces from canada, england, and usa. i have yet to find australian “agee”.

4. the simple, smooth lines.

5. durability. so many have survived all these years. even food-splattered, caked on messes can be cleaned up and made to look new (i use baking soda). and while breaks do happen, no piece is too precious. i use them all, often.

6. they look nice out in the open. (if you disagree, be glad you don’t live with me)

7. a little piece of history. each decade of production had a distinct style.

8. each piece has a personal story behind it: where i found it, how much (or little) i paid, how it followed me home despite our little chat about how very little room i have left in the cupboards.

9. i get my consumerism fix without breaking the bank. most of my pieces cost between two to six dollars each.

10. it’s photogenic.

11. and functional.

12 a) it makes food look even better.

12 b) it makes icky hallowe’en candy (clearly a different category than “food”) look even better.

13. this list was originally entitled “10 reasons i love vintage pyrex” but obviously, i’m incapable of stopping at 10. vintage pyrex also helps clear writer’s block. take note, tara ;)

14. if you adore pyrex enough, you might be asked to join an online pyrex collective. and meet others who have it way worse than you!  check out this little post i wrote for “the pyrex collective” sharing a rare 1958 piece of pyrex i found…

another good resource for learning more about vintage pyrex, pattern names, sizes and so on, is pyrex love.

got any pyrex in your cupboards? got your own reasons? share them below…
