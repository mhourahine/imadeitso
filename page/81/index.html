a little bit of info about the spotted deadnettle:

  • scientific name: Lamium maculatum
  • variety shown above in photo is “pink chablis”
  • member of the mint family
  • low-maintenance perennial ground-cover that grows and spreads quickly
  • part to full shade; tolerates sun
  • deadhead flowers (more about deadheading later this week) to encourage dense growth after the first bloom
  • may have received its name because leaves are similar in shape to the stinging nettle but without the toxic effects
information about deadnettle sourced from marthastewart.com and fine gardening.

recipe: perfectly easy summer avocado dip

Posted on May 28, 2010 by imadeitso

i love this dip. it’s my go-to recipe for something easy, for a casual get-together. when you want to do something more than just open a jar of salsa for your guests…not that there’s anything wrong with that.

most of my tried-and-true recipes don’t have measured amounts of each ingredient. i’m more of an eye-baller for quantities. besides, who has time to measure? you’re lucky i’m making you anything! ;-)

(not with baking, however. that’s a true science involving exact measurements and techniques such as observing the meniscus; good old chemistry geek behaviour in which i’m more than happy to indulge…)

but for things like this dip, the important thing is the combination of flavours in approximate proportions to each other. for the sake of making things easy to follow and re-create, i’ve attempted to measure quantities…

here goes.

ingredients:

  • 3 ripe avocados (slightly soft to the touch, not mushy), seed & skin removed.
  • 1/2 cup cucumber
  • 1 medium tomato, or a handful of grape or cherry tomatoes (darnit, not a handful. maybe… half a cup?)
  • splash (4 tbsp) of lemon juice
  • 2 turns of the salt grinder (1/4 tsp salt)
  • 4 turns of the pepper mill (1/2 tsp pepper)

directions:

i make two versions of this, depending on my mood. sometimes, i just chop everything up by hand for a more chunky version, add the lemon, salt and pepper to taste and gently toss it with a spoon. other times, i use the food processor.

if i’m using the food processor, i don’t throw everything in all at once because i find it takes too long to get the ingredients chopped to the right size, and it all turns into a big mush. i like to process one ingredient at a time, and layer them in a glass bowl. it creates a better consistency, and looks great with those layers of red, green and white.  then, as the final step, i pour the lemon juice on top and add the salt and pepper.

this can be served right away, but i find it tastes even better the second day after being refrigerated overnight.

serve with pita chips or nachos.


yum.

i just made a batch for the weekend. enjoy yours! (the weekend, and the dip if you make it!)

thrift-store find: holly hobbie

Posted on May 27, 2010 by imadeitso

anyone else hear “holly hobbie” and get instantly transported back to their childhood?

i had a holly hobbie sewing machine as a young girl. now that i think of it, i am pretty sure i also had a holly hobbie metal lunchbox and thermos too. mention sewing or holly hobbie to me now all these years later, and i still get a little weak in the knees.

when i came across a vintage holly hobbie plate the other day, i knew exactly where it was going: in our basement (after renovations this month) where we’ll have a little music corner set up.

my husband and i met in highschool, and while we both leaned towards the sciences and maths, we were heavily involved in the music program and participated throughout our years in highschool.

music was a big part of our lives, and it continues to be, just in a different way now.

we listen to all kinds of music, from weird al to wagner (or should i say “veird al to wagner”? see? music geek :)), johnny cash to jamiroquai. we attend concerts in the park during the summer, while the kids run around the folding chairs and picnic blankets spread across the grassy field. on occasion, we open up those dusty black boxes lined in velvet, which hold the instruments from way back when. much like riding a bike, our fingers remember where to go; it’s only our embouchure that needs a bit of work…

we hope that our kids grow up with an appreciation not just of music, but for all that music can bring to life.

i often forget this myself. i’ll be walking around the house in a funk, moody and miserable for some reason or other. a little black raincloud, it happens to all of us sometimes. if i have the presence of mind to flick on some music, the day is saved nine times out of ten.

music, much like a smell or a memory, can instantly whisk me away from where i am. or it fills that space in my mind that sometimes entertains negative chatter.  it can set the tone for a dinner party, or a quiet afternoon alone while the littlest ones are napping. it can also be the cause of a twitchy eyelid, if say, the husband has to listen to beastie boys at full blast on a saturday morning before i’ve had my first cup of coffee… but more often than not, music makes life better.

while the sentiment on this plate is a little too cheerful and sweet for my usual taste, and while i never thought i’d be the kind of person to buy a decorative plate (if i ever buy an elvis or queen elizabeth plate, you have my permission to shoot me never visit my blog again)… well, what can i say? holly hobbie makes me do crazy things.

(1972, mint condition, $1.99)

i made it so: reed diffuser

Posted on May 26, 2010 by imadeitso

i try to be mindful about what chemicals i bring into the home. for example, the majority of cleaning products i use i make myself. most involve some combination of water, vinegar, baking soda and essential oils. i add essential oils because the scent cuts through the sharp vinegar smell (i really don’t like the smell of vinegar), and also because some essential oils have antiseptic properties.

i’m far from perfect though (see “baby oil” below) and there’s always more i can do. one of these days, i’ll share my list of what i use, what works and what conventional products i use because i simply haven’t found a good enough alternative yet. maybe you could share your suggestions with me then.

but today, i want to talk about reed diffusers. i’ve seen these in stores over the last couple of years, and they basically look like a bunch of bamboo sticks in a pretty glass jar. aesthetically pleasing things.

the idea behind them is that the sticks carry the fragrance from the jar up to the top where they “diffuse” into the air. they are touted as being a safer and cleaner alternative to burning scented candles, because there is no flame, and no soot is released.

(as an aside: i like soy-based or beeswax candles, with lead-free wicks. these waxes don’t have petrochemicals and burn cleaner than conventional wax candles.)

i like a pretty smelling house just as much as the next person, but i’ve always hesitated to buy these. most of them have a long list of ingredients, many of which i can pronounce but only thanks to my years of taking organic chemistry through university!  others have only a few ingredients listed, like “alcohol, essential oil, fragrance.”  but all manners of sin and toxicity can be legally hidden under the term “fragrance.”

i decided to try making my own version of these reed diffusers. to work, they need the following:

  • a scent (i used pure essential oil.)

  • something for the fragrance to “stick” to (mineral oil is a good choice; i only had baby oil on hand, which is essentially mineral oil but alas with some “baby powder” scent added. you could also use a light vegetable oil.)
  • something to transfer the fragrant oil up out of the jar (any porous stick or twig will work, as will tightly rolled up pieces of paper; i used bamboo skewers from the dollar store with the sharp tips cut off.)

  • something to help emulsify and carry the oil up the stick. (pure vodka is a good choice; it is stronger than rubbing alcohol, and it makes people wonder what you’re up to.)

  • some vessel to hold this all together; preferably with a narrow opening (i used a vintage milk glass bud vase that i got at the thrift store for 49 cents; the narrow opening helps keep the alcohol from evaporating.)

here are the steps, they’re very complicated so read carefully. you may want to bookmark this page for future reference…

  1. fill the vase about half-way with the oil.
  2. add a splash of vodka
  3. add a few drops of essential oil
  4. put the sticks in the vase

that’s it! (i was joking about it being tricky.)

it takes a little time for the reeds to carry the fragrance up. i got a little impatient and flipped the sticks upside down so the whole length of bamboo was saturated with the oil. i do this periodically anyways when i find the scent is waning, to expose the oil-infused reeds to the air.

i’m going to play around with other essential oils and quantities. now that i look at it, i could have fit more sticks into the vase, which would help carry more scent upwards, as well as help keep the alcohol from evaporating.

so why could i not have just used essential oil? why add mineral or another “carrier” oil? well, pure essential oils are expensive, and i’d need a large quantity of it to saturate the reeds. but also, a little essential oil goes a long way in terms of fragrance. mineral oil helps dilute the scent.

so that’s it.  although the ingredients are more natural than those found in most store-bought diffusers, these are still chemicals that can be dangerous to pets and children.  so i display mine out of reach, on the mantle…

if you make this, let me know how it turns out. happy sniffing!
